Plant a SEED in three steps

Instead of adding a mental health program, attach resilience training to a journey a group is already on. Pick a step to watch it grow.

Find the expedition

Discover the goal your group is already chasing, like a faster 40-yard dash for football players or a higher SAT score for test preppers. If nothing unites the group yet, curate an adventure the whole group can take ownership of.

Choose the assets

Decide which mental wellness strengths to embed. The information on these is everywhere. The delivery is what changes.

Growth mindset Emotional vulnerability Intrinsic motivation Self-image confidence Identity clarity

Plant the SEEDs

Bring the exercises into the thinking, training, planning, preparing, and performing moments people already care about. That is where brains pick up adversity strategies, reframing techniques, and emotional response patterns that stand-alone mental health programs often fail to teach.

Why most wellness programs miss

Wellness programs brought into schools and clubs often produce mixed results, and sometimes cause harm. Our research argues the ideas inside them are not the problem. The way they are delivered is. Hover over a card to compare the clinic with the classroom.

An imposed goal

When the change isn't self-chosen, it can clash with how a person sees themselves. The brain can reject what doesn't fit who they think they are.

In the clinic: you choose what to work on.
In a universal program: someone else decides.
Hover to compare

An imposed setting

Learning scales with how meaningful an experience feels. A program with no connection to what a person cares about often lacks the weight to change anything.

In the clinic: you walk in because it matters to you.
In a universal program: you are required to be there.
Hover to compare

An imposed relationship

A clinician is someone you seek out. A guest facilitator is someone you were handed. Without that voluntary trust, people disengage or quietly resist.

In the clinic: you seek out the clinician.
In a universal program: the facilitator is assigned.

Preprint

Reconceptualizing Universal Mental Health as Salience-Embedded Experiential Development in Adolescence

Universal school-based wellness programs often produce inconsistent results. We argue the cause is a mismatch between clinical and universal settings, not flawed therapeutic methods.

  • Clinics give people agency over the change, the context, and the relationship. Universal programs impose all three.
  • That creates identity friction and lowers salience, the sense that something matters.
  • Embedding resilience training in goals, settings, and relationships adolescents already chose is a strengths-based path that may improve engagement and safety.
